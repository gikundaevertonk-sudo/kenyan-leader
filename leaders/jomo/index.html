Jomo Kenyatta

Prime Minister 1963. President 1964 to 1978. KANU. Died 1978.

What the record shows

  • DImprisoned and restricted by the colonial state from 1952 to 1961 after the Kapenguria trial.
  • DThe KPU was banned in October 1969 after violence during his visit to Kisumu. Oginga Odinga and other KPU leaders were detained.
  • DMP J.M. Kariuki was murdered in March 1975. A parliamentary select committee implicated senior police officers. No one was convicted.
  • DThe Million-Acre Scheme resettled families on former settler farms bought with British and World Bank finance.

What they said, and what they did

  • Said they would'African Socialism' (Sessional Paper No. 10, 1965).
    What they did
    DRan a market economy with private property protected and foreign investment encouraged.
  • Said they wouldFight poverty, ignorance and disease.
    What they did
    ISchools and clinics expanded, much of it through Harambee self-help funding, while regional and class inequality widened (interpretation).
  • Said they wouldIndependence would right the colonial land injustice.
    What they did
    ILand moved by sale, not restitution. Those with capital and connections gained most (interpretation; see the Ndung'u Report).

Where the evidence points, by dimension

Role of the state in the economy: Leans market-led (high confidence)
Protected private property, welcomed foreign capital and Africanised ownership rather than nationalising it, under a blueprint titled 'African Socialism'.
Land, wealth and taxes: Low redistribution (medium confidence)
Settler land was transferred mainly by sale (willing buyer, willing seller) with British-funded loans. The 2004 Ndung'u Commission later documented irregular allocations of public land to the politically connected, beginning in this era.
Institutions and power-sharing: Executive-centred (high confidence)
Amendments from 1964 to 1969 created an executive presidency, stripped the regional (majimbo) assemblies of power, merged the Senate into one chamber and ended in a de facto one-party state.
Political style: Establishment (high confidence)
Built and led the post-independence ruling order.
Civil liberties: Order-first (high confidence)
Banned the opposition KPU in 1969 and detained its leaders without trial. Critics, including the writer Ngugi wa Thiong'o, were detained under public-security laws.

Contradictions in the record

  • IA symbol of the anti-colonial struggle who then detained fellow nationalists who opposed him.
  • ICalled the economy socialist while building one of Africa's more market-friendly states.

What they appear to have stood for

State-centred, conservative nation-building: a strong presidency, a market economy and little room for organised dissent.

How to read this page

Actions come first: laws, votes, decisions, court cases. Words count less, and only where the record is thin and not contradicted. A leader with real power now is judged on actions only.
