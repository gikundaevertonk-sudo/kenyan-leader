Jaramogi Oginga Odinga

Vice President 1964 to 1966. Founded KPU and later co-founded FORD. Died 1994.

Held power before. Actions first; uncontradicted words and reports count less.

Last reviewed: not yet recorded · 1 claim not yet re-checked

What the record shows

  • DResigned the vice presidency in 1966 and formed the Kenya People's Union.
  • DKPU MPs were forced to seek re-election in the 1966 'Little General Election' under a new constitutional amendment.
  • DDetained after the KPU ban in 1969.
  • UTried to register an opposition party in 1982. The attempt was one trigger for the one-party amendment.

What they said, and what they did

  • Said they wouldRefused in 1961 to form a government until Kenyatta was freed.
    What they did
    DKANU held to this, and Kenyatta was released that year.
  • Said they wouldNationalisation, free education and land for the landless (KPU platform, 1966).
    What they did
    INot counted. He held executive power only briefly as Vice President, and KPU was banned in 1969 before it could govern.

Where the evidence points, by dimension

Land, wealth and taxes: Strong redistribution (medium confidence)
Left the government in 1966 over its direction, including a land policy that sold rather than returned land (interpretation of his reasons).
Institutions and power-sharing: Leans checks and devolution (medium confidence)
Co-founded FORD in 1991 to force a return to multiparty politics.
Political style: Anti-establishment (high confidence)
Resigned as Vice President in 1966 and formed the first post-independence opposition party.
Civil liberties: Leans liberty-first (medium confidence)
Kept organising opposition through detention (1969 to 1971) and later house arrest.

Contradictions in the record

  • IServed as Vice President in the government whose direction he then rejected.

What they appear to have stood for

Left-leaning nationalist and opposition pioneer. His economic programme was never tested in office, so it is not counted.

How to read this page

Actions come first: laws, votes, decisions, court cases. Words count less, and only where the record is thin and not contradicted. A leader with real power now is judged on actions only.
