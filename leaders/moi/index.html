Daniel arap Moi

Vice President 1967 to 1978. President 1978 to 2002. KANU. Died 2020.

Held power before. Actions first; uncontradicted words and reports count less.

What the record shows

  • DSection 2A repealed in December 1991. Won the 1992 and 1997 multiparty elections.
  • DHanded power to Mwai Kibaki in December 2002 after KANU lost.
  • DThe Goldenberg export-compensation scandal of the early 1990s happened under his government.
  • DPolice broke up the Saba Saba multiparty rallies of July 1990.

What they said, and what they did

  • Said they would'Nyayo': follow Kenyatta's footsteps with 'peace, love and unity'.
    What they did
    DNarrowed political space further: one-party law, detentions and torture of dissidents.
  • Said they wouldCare for ordinary children and families.
    What they did
    DIntroduced free school milk in 1979 and the 8-4-4 education system in 1985.
  • Said they wouldMultiparty politics would divide the country along tribal lines.
    What they did
    ARepealed Section 2A in December 1991 under domestic and donor pressure. Ethnic clashes followed around the 1992 and 1997 elections, which rights groups linked to KANU figures (attributed).

Where the evidence points, by dimension

Role of the state in the economy: Mixed (medium confidence)
Expanded parastatals and state marketing boards in the 1980s, then under donor pressure liberalised prices, maize marketing and foreign exchange in the early 1990s. The record points both ways.
Land, wealth and taxes: Low redistribution (medium confidence)
The 2004 Ndung'u Commission documented widespread illegal allocation of public land, peaking in the 1980s and 1990s, largely to the politically connected.
Institutions and power-sharing: Executive-centred (high confidence)
Made Kenya a legal one-party state (Section 2A) in 1982, introduced queue voting in 1988 and removed security of tenure for judges and the Attorney General in 1988.
Political style: Establishment (high confidence)
Ran the ruling order for 24 years.
Civil liberties: Order-first (high confidence)
The Truth, Justice and Reconciliation Commission documented detention without trial, torture in the Nyayo House cells and the 1984 Wagalla killings by security forces.

Contradictions in the record

  • IPromised peace, love and unity while presiding over detention and torture of opponents.
  • IResisted multiparty democracy for a decade, then accepted a term limit and handed over power peacefully.

What they appear to have stood for

Authoritarian, patronage-based centralism that bent only under sustained pressure.

How to read this page

Actions come first: laws, votes, decisions, court cases. Words count less, and only where the record is thin and not contradicted. A leader with real power now is judged on actions only.
