Okiya Omtatah

Busia Senator since 2022. Public-interest litigant. Leader of the National Reconstruction Alliance. Weighing a 2027 presidential run.

Never held executive power. Actions first; stated positions and reports count less.

What the record shows

  • DCo-petitioner in the case in which the High Court declared the Housing Levy in the Finance Act 2023 unconstitutional in November 2023.
  • DFiled petitions against the privatisation of Kenya Pipeline Company. Interim orders were refused in February 2026 and the case continues.
  • DGazetted a presidential exploratory committee in November 2024. Said in June 2026 he would announce his decision after receiving its report in early July. A formal declaration was not confirmed in this pass.
  • DThe Supreme Court on 29 October 2024 partly allowed an appeal and declared the Finance Act 2023 constitutional, overturning the Court of Appeal's earlier ruling against it. The Housing Levy fight he helped start therefore did not end in a lasting court win.

Where the evidence points, by dimension

Institutions and power-sharing: Checks and devolution (high confidence)
Has brought many constitutional petitions against government actions. His challenge to the Finance Act 2023, with others, led the High Court to strike down the Housing Levy in November 2023. The levy was later re-enacted under a separate housing law.
Role of the state in the economy: Leans state-led (medium confidence)
Went to court to stop the sale of Kenya Pipeline Company, arguing the privatisation was driven by IMF pressure. The High Court declined interim orders in February 2026.
Political style: Anti-establishment (medium confidence)
An independent-minded senator who says he will run even as a 'lone ranger' and faults the opposition for having no agenda beyond removing Ruto.
Civil liberties: Leans liberty-first (low confidence)
Publicly condemned the arrest of MP Peter Salasya and vowed to fight it.

Contradictions in the record

  • IWins in the High Court were later reversed or overtaken on appeal (interpretation). The Housing Levy came back under a separate law.

What they appear to have stood for

A checks-and-balances litigant who uses the courts against the executive and resists the sale of state assets.
