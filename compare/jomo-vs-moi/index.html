Jomo Kenyatta vs Daniel arap Moi

They differ most on role of the state in the economy. They sit close on land, wealth and taxes, institutions and power-sharing, political style, civil liberties.

Role of the state in the economyLeans market-led
Why

Protected private property, welcomed foreign capital and Africanised ownership rather than nationalising it, under a blueprint titled 'African Socialism'. (high confidence)

Mixed
Why

Expanded parastatals and state marketing boards in the 1980s, then under donor pressure liberalised prices, maize marketing and foreign exchange in the early 1990s. The record points both ways. (medium confidence)

Land, wealth and taxesLow redistribution
Why

Settler land was transferred mainly by sale (willing buyer, willing seller) with British-funded loans. The 2004 Ndung'u Commission later documented irregular allocations of public land to the politically connected, beginning in this era. (medium confidence)

Low redistribution
Why

The 2004 Ndung'u Commission documented widespread illegal allocation of public land, peaking in the 1980s and 1990s, largely to the politically connected. (medium confidence)

Executive-centred
Why

Made Kenya a legal one-party state (Section 2A) in 1982, introduced queue voting in 1988 and removed security of tenure for judges and the Attorney General in 1988. (high confidence)

Civil libertiesOrder-first
Why

Banned the opposition KPU in 1969 and detained its leaders without trial. Critics, including the writer Ngugi wa Thiong'o, were detained under public-security laws. (high confidence)

Order-first
Why

The Truth, Justice and Reconciliation Commission documented detention without trial, torture in the Nyayo House cells and the 1984 Wagalla killings by security forces. (high confidence)

Who they are

Jomo Kenyatta: Prime Minister 1963. President 1964 to 1978. KANU. Died 1978. State-centred, conservative nation-building: a strong presidency, a market economy and little room for organised dissent.

Daniel arap Moi: Vice President 1967 to 1978. President 1978 to 2002. KANU. Died 2020. Authoritarian, patronage-based centralism that bent only under sustained pressure.
