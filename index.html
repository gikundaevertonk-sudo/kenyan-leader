The record, leader by leader

Research draft built from public reporting up to 30 September 2026. Each line is tagged so you can tell facts from claims from interpretation. Open a leader to see what they said they would do next to what they did.

How this works

There are two quizzes. Every leader compares you with everyone in this draft, from the independence generation to people in office today. Current climate compares you only with the people shaping politics now and in the race for 2027. Both use the same six dimensions, so a result in one can be read next to the other.

The core rule: power is judged by action

Every leader is sorted into one of two groups.

  • Has held executive power (President, Vice or Deputy President, Prime Minister, Cabinet Minister or Cabinet Secretary, Governor). Placements come only from what they did: laws signed or blocked, decisions, spending, appointments, and how they treated courts, media and opponents. Their promises appear in a "said / did" list so you can see the gap, but promises never move their placement. The code drops any words-based placement for this group.
  • Has not held executive power (MPs, senators, assistant ministers, activists, aspirants). Their votes, bills and actions come first. Where those are thin, their stated positions and manifestos can be used, tagged S and drawn as dotted circles.

Self-descriptions and party labels are never evidence. Kenya's 1965 blueprint was called "African Socialism" while it protected private property and courted foreign investors. Here, the policy counts and the label does not.

The six dimensions

    Reading the tags

    • D Documented: directly supported by a reliable report, a court record or a public record.
    • A Attributed: a claim made by a named source such as a journalist, group or rival. Not established fact.
    • I Interpretation: our reading of several actions together. Open to challenge.
    • U Not re-checked in this pass: written from general knowledge and needs verification before you rely on it.
    • S Stated position: what someone who has not held executive power says they would do. Used only for that group.

    Matching

    Your answers give you a position on each dimension, from -2 to +2. A leader is compared with you only on dimensions where the evidence supports a placement, and a leader needs at least three of them to appear in your matches. The others stay in Records.

    Solid evidence counts for more than thin evidence. Before your position is compared with a leader's, the leader's placement is pulled toward the neutral midpoint by its confidence: high confidence keeps it as is, medium keeps 80% of it and low keeps 50%. A leader with thin evidence therefore looks closer to neutral and cannot outrank a well-evidenced one just by sitting on your exact position.

    For each dimension, the overlap is 100% when you and the leader are at the same point and falls in a straight line to 0% when they are 3 points apart. A leader's overall bar is the average of those overlaps, so opposite positions read near 0% instead of being squeezed into the top half. The main figure beside each bar is how many dimensions you are close on, meaning within 1 point of the leader's placement before any pull toward the midpoint. The headline match also needs enough evidence overall, roughly three medium-confidence dimensions.

    Limits

    • Current profiles reflect reporting up to 30 September 2026. Alliances move fast.
    • Historical profiles were drafted from the established historical record, with reference overviews linked as starting points. A few details are tagged U and still need checking against primary sources.
    • Some leaders had long careers across very different roles. A single placement per dimension flattens that, so read the profile.
    • A court allegation is not a finding. Where an outcome was not verified, the profile says so.
