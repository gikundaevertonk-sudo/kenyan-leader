Questions about the Kenya 2027 election quiz

What is Siasa Compass?

A free Kenya politics quiz. You answer 18 questions, and it shows which Kenyan leaders' documented records sit closest to your views on six policy dimensions, from independence to the 2027 race.

How does it score leaders?

Actions count most: laws, votes, court cases and decisions. Words count less, and only where the record is thin and not contradicted. Leaders with too little evidence stay out of your matches. See the Method tab for the full rules.

Is it biased, or voting advice?

No. It does not rank, predict or endorse anyone. Every claim is tagged by how well it is evidenced and linked to sources. It is a research draft, so check the sources and decide for yourself.

Which leaders are covered?

Current figures in government, opposition and the 2027 race, plus earlier leaders such as Jomo Kenyatta, Daniel arap Moi, Mwai Kibaki and Raila Odinga. Browse all leader profiles.

The record, leader by leader

Public reporting up to 30 Sep 2026. Open a leader to compare what they said with what they did.

How this works

Current climate compares you with today's contenders. Every leader adds everyone since independence. Both use the same six dimensions.

The core rule

  • Actions come first. Laws, votes, spending, appointments, court cases, and how leaders treated courts, media and opponents.
  • Words count less. Where actions are thin, reported manifestos, speeches and interviews can place a leader. Tagged S, drawn dotted, weighted at 60%.
  • Reports count least. For people whose job is mostly words, such as MPs and senators, reported or attributed claims can fill a gap too. Tagged H (hearsay), weighted at 40%, never used where actions exist.
  • Contradicted words are dropped. They stay visible in "said / did", marked as contradicted.
  • Real power now? Only actions count. The President and sitting governors can act, so their promises and hearsay never count. With too little action on record, they stay out of your matches.
  • Little power of their own? Some grace. Someone in office with limited power, such as the Deputy President, can still be placed by their words, at the lower weight.

Labels are never evidence. Kenya's 1965 "African Socialism" paper protected private property. The policy counts, not the name.

    What else sways votes

    Other things still decide many votes. This quiz matches you on views about how Kenya should be run, so it treats them differently:

    • Tribe and regional kingpins. Ethnic voting is still strong, but only 4 in 10 Kenyans say their community has a recognised political leader (TIFA, September 2025). The quiz never asks your ethnicity or matches on it. It does ask whether a community should vote as a bloc, and whether being close to the government of the day brings development: the "our turn to eat" idea.
    • Money and handouts. In a July 2026 NCCK study, 91% said candidates cannot win without giving cash or gifts, and 94% said money strongly sways results. Handouts are not a policy, so they are not matched on, but documented harambees, donations and appeals to ethnic loyalty appear in profiles under "Handouts and ethnic appeals".
    • Party waves, coalitions, charisma, fame, fear and rumours. None of these is a view about how the country should be run, so none is matched on. Alliances and defections are in each leader's record.

    Reading the tags

    • D Documented: backed by a reliable report or public record.
    • A Attributed: a named source's claim, not established fact.
    • I Interpretation: our reading. Open to challenge.
    • U Unchecked: needs verification.
    • S Stated position: a promise from someone not in power, weighted below actions.
    • H Hearsay: a report or claim about what someone did or said, not verified. Lightest weight.

    Matching

    Your answers place you from -2 to +2 on each dimension. Leaders need evidence on at least four of the six dimensions to appear in your matches. Others stay in Records.

    Thin evidence is pulled toward neutral: high confidence keeps 100%, medium 80%, low 50%. So a weak record can't outrank a solid one.

    Overlap is 100% at the same point and 0% at 3 points apart. The bar is the average, with stated positions at 60% and hearsay at 40%. "Close on" counts dimensions within 1 point. Your headline match is always #1.

    Your answers

    When you finish a quiz, your answers, where they place you and your top three matches are added to an anonymous tally, so we can see how visitors answer each question. Nothing that identifies you is stored: no name, no location, no IP address, only the date. Untick the box under the questions to leave yours out.

    Limits

    • Current profiles run to 30 September 2026. Alliances move fast.
    • Some historical details are tagged U and still need checking.
    • One placement per dimension flattens long careers. Read the profile.
    • An allegation is not a finding.

    About this Kenya politics quiz

    Siasa Compass is a free quiz about Kenyan politics. You answer 18 short statements, agreeing or disagreeing with each, and the quiz shows which Kenyan leaders' records sit closest to your views on six dimensions: the economy, land and wealth, social values, institutions and devolution, political style, and civil liberties.

    Two quiz modes: Current climate and Every leader

    Current climate compares you with the people shaping Kenyan politics now: the government, the opposition and those in the race for the 2027 election. Every leader compares you with everyone in this draft, from the independence generation to leaders in office today.

    How are leaders placed?

    • Actions come first. Laws signed, votes, spending, appointments and court cases set a leader's placement wherever that evidence exists.
    • Statements count less. Where the record is thin, documented manifestos, speeches and interviews can place a leader, weighted below actions. Promises contradicted by the record are not counted, and leaders with real power now are judged on actions only.
    • Sources are tagged. Each line in a leader's record is marked as documented, attributed to a named source, our interpretation, a stated position, or not yet re-checked, so you can tell facts from claims.

    Does Siasa Compass rank or endorse anyone?

    No. It is a research draft built from public reporting up to 30 September 2026. It does not rank leaders, predict the 2027 election or endorse any politician or party. A match means your answers overlap with a leader's documented record, not that you should vote for them.

    Where can I read Kenyan leaders' records?

    The Records page lists every leader in the draft with what they said they would do next to what they did, the evidence behind each placement, and links to sources. Leaders with too little evidence are left out of matches rather than guessed at.
