The record, leader by leader

Research draft built from public reporting up to 30 September 2026. Each line is tagged so you can tell facts from claims from interpretation. Open a leader to see the reasoning and sources.

How this works

Each leader is placed on six dimensions using a fixed order of evidence: what they did in office, the policies and laws they backed or opposed, how they treated institutions and opponents, the documented effects, then their repeated public positions. Party ideology and self-description come last.

The six dimensions

    Reading the tags

    • D Documented: directly supported by a reliable report, a court record or a public record.
    • A Attributed: a claim made by a named source such as a journalist, group or rival. Not established fact.
    • I Interpretation: our reading of several actions together. Open to challenge.
    • U Not re-checked in this pass: written from general knowledge and needs verification before you rely on it.

    Matching

    Your answers give you a position on each dimension. A leader is compared with you only on dimensions where the evidence supports a placement, and solid evidence counts for more than thin evidence. A leader needs at least three comparable dimensions to appear in your matches. The others stay in the Leaders tab.

    Limits

    • Kenyan politics moves fast. Alliances and parties in this draft reflect reporting up to 30 September 2026.
    • Many placements rest on a small number of documented actions. Dashed circles mark low confidence.
    • A court allegation is not a finding. Where an outcome was not verified, the profile says so.
    • Media sources have their own interests. Where possible profiles cite more than one outlet, but this is a first pass, not a finished audit.

    Siasa Compass is a research draft. It documents political behaviour and does not rank leaders, predict elections or endorse any politician or party. Spotted an error or a missing source? Send it in and the profile can be corrected.
